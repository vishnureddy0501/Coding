# 0. Why Do We Need Aviso When We Already Have a CRM?

## Simple explanation

A CRM such as Salesforce is mainly a **system of record**. It stores information entered by sales teams, such as accounts, opportunities, deal stages, amounts, close dates, and activities.

Aviso uses that CRM data along with other signals to provide **intelligence and guidance**. It helps sales teams understand:

- Which deals are likely to close
- Which deals are at risk or have become inactive
- Whether the team is likely to meet its revenue target
- How the current forecast compares with historical trends and AI predictions
- Which deals or accounts require attention
- What changed in the pipeline and why

```text
CRM:   Stores what the sales team entered
Aviso: Analyzes the data and helps the team decide what to do next
```

## What does Aviso add?

CRM data can be spread across many records and may depend heavily on manual updates. Aviso brings the important information together and adds:

- AI-based deal scores and revenue forecasts
- Deal-risk and pipeline-change signals
- Forecast roll-ups across representatives, managers, and regions
- Historical comparisons and trends
- Information from engagement and other connected systems
- Dashboards that help managers prioritize decisions

For example, the CRM may say:

```text
Deal amount: ₹50 lakh
Stage: Negotiation
Close date: October 30
```

Aviso can use this information with activity history, past behavior, engagement, and pipeline changes to indicate:

```text
This deal is at risk because the close date moved twice,
customer engagement decreased, and there has been no recent activity.
```

## Can writeback be done directly in the CRM?

Yes. Users can update fields directly in the CRM. **Writeback is not the main reason for using Aviso.**

Writeback allows a user to update CRM information from Aviso without switching applications. Aviso sends the change back to the CRM so the CRM remains the source of truth.

```text
User changes a deal in Aviso
              ↓
Aviso validates and sends the update
              ↓
CRM stores the updated value
```

The benefit is that users can review Aviso's insights and take action from the same screen. The important value is the intelligence, prioritization, and forecasting around the CRM data—not merely editing CRM fields.

## Interview-ready answer

> A CRM is mainly the system of record where account, opportunity, activity, and pipeline data is stored. Aviso works as an intelligence layer on top of that data. It combines CRM and engagement signals to identify deal risks, generate AI-based scores and forecasts, show pipeline changes, and help managers decide where to focus.
>
> We could update fields directly in the CRM, and the CRM remains the source of truth. Aviso's writeback feature simply lets users make those updates without leaving the workflow. The main value of Aviso is not writeback; it is converting raw CRM data into predictions, risks, priorities, and actions.
