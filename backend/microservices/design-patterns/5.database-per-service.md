# Database per Service Pattern — Interview Notes

## What Is It?

Each microservice owns its data. Other services cannot directly read or modify its tables.

```text
Order Service   → Order Database
Payment Service → Payment Database
User Service    → User Database
```

Services exchange data through APIs or events.

The databases do not have to run on different servers. The important rule is clear ownership and access isolation.

## Why Is It Used?

A shared database tightly couples services. One service may depend on another service's schema or change its data without following its business rules.

Database per Service allows each service to:

- Change its schema independently
- Deploy with less coordination
- Choose a suitable database technology
- Scale its storage independently
- Protect its business rules

## Drawbacks

- Cross-service joins are difficult.
- Transactions across services require patterns such as Saga.
- Reports may require replicated data or CQRS read models.
- Data becomes eventually consistent in many workflows.

## Interview Answer

> Database per Service gives each microservice ownership of its data. Other services access that data only through APIs or events. This improves independence and protects service boundaries, but cross-service queries and transactions become more complex.
